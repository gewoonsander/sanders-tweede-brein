Stap voor stap.
De toekomst en de effecten.
Maar projectmanagement tools denken in objecten.
Workspaces, folders, lists, tasks, subtasks.
Er is geen automatische afbeelding tussen die twee mentale modelen.
Research shows that teams with clear structural frameworks
before tool implementation
have 3.5 times higher adoption rates
than teams who figure it out as they go.
Because when your team understands
the why behind the structure,
why this is a list versus a folder,
why go sit at the top,
the how becomes obvious.
And here's what compounds this.
Every tool uses different terminology.
What ClickUp calls a list,
Asana calls a project.
What Notion calls a database,
ClickUp calls a table view.
So people jump from tool to tool,
thinking the next one will be
magically solve their problems.
And the real issue is,
they don't have a universal language
for their work structure.
The issue isn't learning every feature of your tool.
The real issue is understanding your work structure
so clearly that you can build it in any tool.
So here's the framework that changes everything.
The three-step translation process
we use to build single source of truth in any tool.
I use ClickUp as the example,
but these principles work in Asana, Monday, Notion
or whatever you're using.
First, map your I-Core output elements
to your tool structure.
Here's how it works in ClickUp specifically.
Your goals become ClickUp goals.
That's a dedicated object that sits above everything else.
Your projects, work streams and operations
all become folders.
Inside each folder you create lists
for the specific workflows.
Your tasks become ClickUp tasks.
That's it. Don't overcomplicate it.
In Asana it looks different,
but follows the same logic.
Goals are portfolios, projects are projects,
tasks are tasks.
In Monday the same hierarchy, different names.
The tool doesn't matter,
the mapping principle does.
Second, replicate your process map
as a workflow inside your tool.
Remember the client sales call workflow
from the process mapping video?
Create a list called Sales Pipeline.
Set up statuses that match your workflow steps.
Offer call, booked, prep, in progress,
follow up, closed.
Now every task flows through these statuses
visually at a list view.
So you can see everything sequentially.
Same data, different perspectives.
This is your process map brought to life.
The key is using your tools, custom views,
to show the same work in different ways
based on what each person needs to see.
Third, implement single source of truth
through context and communication.
This is where most teams completely fail.
They build the perfect structure
and all the actual communication still happens
in Slack, Microsoft Teams, Google Chat,
and email.
Here's the rule.
Every conversation about a task
happens in that task's comments.
Every decision gets documented there.
Every file gets attached there.
Your tool's notification center,
ClickUp's inbox, Asana's inbox,
Monday's notification,
becomes your command center.
We check our ClickUp inbox first thing every morning.
It shows us exactly what needs our attention.
No searching through Slack threads.
No searching through email.
Everything in one place.
This exact structure I implemented in BigCorporate

inside Asana without anything falling through the cracks.
